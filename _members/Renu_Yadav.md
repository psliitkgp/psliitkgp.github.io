At present, she is a Research Associate at University of Bristol. She completed her PhD in from Department of Physics, Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur, India. 

She works in the field of optics and complex photonics. She fabricates micro- and nanostructures—such as optofluidic devices, microchannels, photonic crystals, and holograms—using optical photolithography, soft lithography, the sol-gel method, holography, and laser writing techniques. She probes light–matter interactions using random lasing in quasi-periodic and disordered structures. Additionally, she utilizes the finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) method to simulate these structures and verify their experimentally observed behavior. Her work elucidates the nature of light–matter interactions in complex systems and finds applications in nanoscale perturbation tracking, dequenching plasmon-quenched fluorophore emission, understanding the interaction of lasing modes with plasmonic nanocavities, and developing on-chip tunable single-mode laser sources and temperature sensors.
