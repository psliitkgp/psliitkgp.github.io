During his Ph.D. journey, he specialized in the design and fabrication of optoelectronic devices developing expertise in crafting PDMS lenses and polymer-based gratings using techniques such as soft lithography, UV photolithography, laser writing, and holography. He studied and characterized the gratings using interference birefringence and diffraction principles. To design and fabricate these optoelectronic devices, tools and software used were SolidWorks, AutoCAD, Comsol, Lumerical, LabVIEW, MATLAB, Python, and machine learning. 

Additionally, he utilized Zemax and Dialux for optical simulations and Kicad for PCB design. His hands-on experience extended to the integration of microcontrollers and microprocessors like Arduino and Raspberry Pi. 

During his M-tech final year, he worked on a project called *"Visible light communication with dynamic source and detector/(LiFi)"* and applied for patent with  Patent application no. 201931054615 dated 31/12/2019 *“Digital locking device and its method of encrypted bidirectional serial data communication between LED's”*.
